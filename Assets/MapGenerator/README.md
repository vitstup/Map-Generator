# Tactical map heightmap workbench

## Agriculture: Sengoku fields and farmsteads

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Set Up Agriculture` imports the agriculture source as readable, linear R16, prepares five PolygonSamuraiEmpire houses with individual three-level mesh LODs, two Synty fence sections and support metadata, upgrades old settings and connects the existing density-overlay toggle. `Generate Agriculture` controls generation independently of overlay visibility. `Rebuild Agriculture Preview` saves the current selection, including compact PNG far masks and owned distance/mesh resources.

Layout version 10 uses 600 m source-anchored districts with seeded position jitter, a broad range of group dimensions and recursive subdivision into 2–7 differently proportioned parcels. About 60% of eligible groups attempt to omit one or two corner parcels; omission is accepted only if the remaining parcels stay connected and their combined outline is irregular. Unmodified groups remain rectangular. Field Area Scale defaults to 0.45: measured cultivated area is about 45% of the preceding version at the two reference selections; field count is a separate budget. Density below 0.04 is rejected; the remap reaches full strength at 0.6, with exponent 1.6. Area/density budgets cap the standard 20 x 20 km map at 500 farmsteads and 2400 fields. Near-zero regions cannot accumulate hundreds of random successes: mean effective density below 0.02 caps output at ten fields and two farms, and true zero produces none. Budgets do not force construction on unsuitable terrain. Nine slope/shore samples and conservative road-footprint checks validate fields. Farm sites choose a suitable group side and prefer roads; cubic access paths are retained only after checking their actual curved route against terrain and parcels. Existing road-generation settings are preserved.

Terrain coverage uses two independent representations. Three mipmapped 1024-square, coverage-premultiplied far-color textures supply distant area averages. In the main terrain plus 600 m, sparse 128 m tiles with one-texel borders store signed contour distance at 1 m spacing. RG packs a signed 16-bit distance over -32..32 m (about 1 mm value precision); BA holds a nearest-sampled 16-bit profile ID. Linear distance decoding commutes with bilinear filtering, while parcel angle, type, size and palette are loaded separately at full precision. No interpolated type/angle values or square cell-noise tinting remain. Albedo detail uses two differently oriented source-soil samples, with detail normals and row shading aligned to the planting grid. Terrain field shading uses soil with no painted green or gold canopy film, including the far masks. Yard holes preserve the original terrain rather than a flat replacement primitive. Far masks use about 16 MiB; sparse distance tiles add 67,600 bytes per occupied tile, plus a small page/profile table.

Field contours are shared by coverage, rounded banks and plant clipping. Embedded farm yards cut a full notch into the outer bank. The notch follows both side fences and the rear fence, leaving the whole frontage open; its narrow outer toe aligns with the actual yard fence rather than the inflated crop or gate mask. AgricultureBuilder exposes Generate Field Banks, enabled by default, independently of courtyard fences and crops. Banks have embedded toes, sloping sides and a raised crown, joined at rounded corners. Textured world-space earth, detail normals, moss variation, sparse low-poly tufts and shadows replace the former solid-color strips. Bank vertices sample terrain once and are reused across strips. Geometry is combined into 256 m spatial cells. Plants are clipped inside the bank, including rounded corners. All fields are dry: no flooded crop state, water geometry or paddy-bed lowering is generated.

Farmsteads use mixed placement, targeting 40% embedded yards among successfully accepted farms (Field Buildings Chance defaults to 0.4). A running quota selects the preferred layout; terrain and collision validation can fall back to the other layout. Embedded yards sit inside the outer edge of one intact parcel, while standalone yards sit beyond the original field-block bounds without cutting or rejecting the group's own parcels. Layouts contain one or two buildings with seeded selection among five prefabs and small scale variation. The 500-farm cap counts farmsteads, not individual buildings. Buildings and fences reject foreign yards and fields. Per-parcel crop exclusions keep plants out of embedded yards and short approaches. The terrain signed-distance representation and far coverage subtract the same yard rectangle, exposing the original neighbouring Terrain layers instead of field soil or a fallow-field replacement. Parcels are never decomposed around buildings, and embedded farms are retained only when their host field is accepted. Beach exclusion and global-road settings remain unchanged.

House variants preserve source material appearance and individual silhouettes. LOD0 retains combined source geometry; LOD1/2 use editor-baked, manifold-checked quadric edge collapse, retaining open boundaries and large wall/roof faces. Source palette UVs and normal islands are retained. Each LOD is one MeshRenderer with material submeshes, reducing object/culling overhead without replacing the original materials. Per-house LODs use screen thresholds 0.09/0.025/0.0004 and animated crossfade. Generated copies of the Synty Shader Graphs enable URP LOD crossfade through Shader Graph's graph/target serialization APIs; original shaders and materials are unchanged. Prepared footprints drive local terrain pads. Structural metadata records exactly four outer platform corners and the lowest platform height. Every near and distant house is positioned with the platform underside 0.18 m above its sampled terrain, independent of source pivot, yaw and scale. Existing source legs extend into the ground; generated posts are restricted to those four corners. Mirrored source modules retain Unity native mesh-combination winding handling; a regression checks the resulting face orientation. Outside the main terrain plus 200 m, only the individual far mesh hierarchy is instantiated, without a close hierarchy or collider.

Wood/rope and bamboo Synty fence sections enclose whole or partial yards, leaving a gate aligned with the access point. Sections follow the terrain slope, reject extreme individual steps, and combine by material in 128 m cells. Each mesh has a unique persisted resource name and distance culling; no per-section GameObject or collider is retained. Construction edits affect the existing terrain height array before one upload, without painting the yard or changing generator heightmap textures.

Connection Radius defaults to 6000 m from the current map center; zero disables agricultural access and a larger radius covers the horizon. Maximum Access Length defaults to 1800 m. Field-only groups also receive access candidates. A spatial global-road lookup, direct-route check and bounded local A* (12 m cells, at most 5000 iterations per route) avoid exhaustive whole-map searches. Line-of-sight reduction and checked cubic corner rounding produce continuous tracks. Routes reject water/beach, excessive grade, buildings, parcels and entire foreign courtyard rectangles. Exact segment/yard tests include road width, shoulder and edge-noise clearance; only a short, owned gate corridor may enter its own yard. Fences and routing share gate dimensions, and nearby groups can join a completed shared access. Access curves are rasterized as type-2 roads using the existing global small-road material, normals, width and shoulder; the separate farm-soil paint is omitted. Global rasterization and incremental access updates share RoadRasterizer. Unchanged tile arrays are reused; at most two hardware-sized texture-array banks retain the combined road coverage, including the horizon. Impossible or over-budget connections are omitted rather than crossing blocked terrain.

Grass coverage excludes fields and paths but does not erase whole yards or expand small house footprints at coverage-map resolution. Building footprints are indexed into grass residency chunks and tested exactly when the GPU creates plants; at most 64 nearby footprints are bound per chunk. No per-frame global building scan or additional persistent GPU allocation is introduced.

Courtyard props use thirteen prepared Synty variants: barrels, baskets, a bucket, wood, a winnowing basket, cart, well, pots and stool. Seeded household sets and small clusters vary the assortment and placement. Yards request roughly 6–18 items according to building count and available space, including embedded yards; collisions can reduce the accepted count. Placement avoids buildings, gates, crops, roads, fences and other props. FarmPropRenderer retains instanced batches and CPU frustum/distance culling, with default draw/shadow ranges of 180/45 m and a map placement cap of 2400. There is no per-prop GameObject, Update or collider. Saved previews retain placements and library references.

Crop clumps receive a deterministic offset of at most 6.5 cm along each parcel axis. Distant clumps retain all positions of the close 0.8 m planting grid and use the same world-anchored jitter; terrain height and crop/yard clipping are evaluated after the offset.

Rice remains two indirect GPU draws per camera: 25-clump/175-triangle close patches and 25-clump/75-triangle simplified patches, covering 4 x 4 m each. Default close/full geometry ranges are 110/1200 m. A 32 m CPU/GPU selection overlap covers the full spatial LOD crossfade; the distant mesh preserves density and approximately preserves blade coverage. Geometry fades gradually over 660–1200 m, without a painted canopy plane. Two bounded camera caches, GPU frustum/distance culling, and Terrain fallback for extra active views remain; no per-plant objects, colliders, normal-loop GPU readback or per-frame matrix uploads are created. Meadow, flowers, trees and rocks respect the agricultural exclusion index. Agriculture results are cached against source content, full height samples, road geometry/clearance, location, settings and footprints; canceled work cannot publish a mismatched cache key/result.

Independent heightmap levels and biome rows now run in bounded parallel CPU work. Terrain construction caches dimensions and directly copies matching square samples; bank construction caches terrain transforms and shares ring samples. Unity texture/object creation remains on the main thread. `Verify Height Generation`, `Verify Biome Generation` and `Verify Terrain Construction` exercise the unchanged rectangular, cancellation, deterministic and resource-lifetime contracts.

`Verify Agriculture Visibility` checks matching clump counts across LODs and bank/fence contour alignment. The close-capture fixture also compares rendered crop signals at 80–1300 m and builds with field banks disabled to check that crops and courtyard fences remain. Reports and captures are in `Logs/AgricultureVisibility` and `Logs/AgricultureRevision`.

`Verify Agriculture Variation` checks mixed outline shapes, connectivity, deterministic omissions, near/far yard holes, removal of canopy ground tint and shared near/far plant roots. Reports are in `Logs/AgricultureVariation`.

`Verify Agriculture Details` checks exactly four corner anchors/post feet, mirrored winding, whole-yard and gate collisions, real embedded rows, shared small-road rendering, prop placement and the rendered overflow road bank. Reports are in `Logs/AgricultureDetail`. The close-capture fixture includes an intact embedded parcel, dry fields and near/far prop culling.

`Verify Agriculture Settlements` checks real high/low-density populations, one- and two-building layouts, area reduction, varied group areas, beach rejection, yard reservations, individual LOD reduction, fences, field-only road connections and connection-radius behavior. Its results and a live Play Mode report are saved in `Logs/AgricultureSettlement`.

`Verify Agriculture` checks deterministic output, low/zero density, varying areas, road/curve exclusion, cancellation, shaders, house LOD/collider budgets and coverage. `Verify Agriculture Revision` measures two real dense selections and a mountain selection, checks population budgets, sub-texel contour accuracy and dry fields. `Capture Agriculture Revision Details` builds an isolated preview scene for close bank/dry-field/soil/path/grounding captures without editing the live map. Reports and images: `Logs/AgricultureRevision`. `Capture And Profile Agriculture` retains matched crop/whole-system comparisons in `Logs/Agriculture`. Synchronized offscreen Editor rendering/readback is not gameplay FPS or isolated GPU time; use a target player build for final rendering budgets.

## Roads

`RoadGenerator` on MapGenerator reads the aligned 4096 x 4096 `japan_roads_RG_4096.png`: black is empty, red is a large road, green is a medium road. Import as readable, linear, uncompressed RGBA32 at full resolution. The source stripe thickness is symbolic. Full-map thinning and tracing preserve junctions, loops and road types; a cached worker task is recreated after Unity hot reload. Source pixels outside the selection provide route context.

In the Editor, a versioned binary topology cache in `Library/TacticalMaps/RoadNetworks` also survives Play-mode domain reloads and Editor restarts. Its key includes the full classified source fingerprint and dimensions. Missing/corrupt cache entries rebuild from the PNG; players keep the in-memory worker cache.

Generation selects source paths by cached bounds, then searches bounded corridors on the actual tactical heightmaps using a coarse grid and cached relief analysis. Selected routes receive exact width checks; difficult passages fall back to a finer search. Length, longitudinal/cross slope, turns and deviation from the reference contribute to routing. Validated cubic curves add smooth, seeded alignment variation; width varies modestly around its configured value. A failed decorative curve falls back to a validated alignment. Water splits routes into separate dry runs; there are no bridges or fords. Impassable sections are omitted and counted in the workbench status. The same source, seed and settings produce the same result. Changing the selected terrain can change the final terrain-aware detour. Heights are not modified.

Inspector controls: Generate Roads, source, large/medium widths (the scene setup uses 8/3 m, 2 m minimum), shoulder, object clearance, slope limits, permitted deviation, analysis spacing, avoidance, seed, per-class wander, bend length and width variation. Generation returns CPU-only `RoadData` and cancellable 128 m sparse distance tiles at one metre spacing, with one-texel borders. Masks rasterize segment-local rectangles in bounded parallel work, rather than testing every segment at every tile pixel. A result cache keys the complete height samples, source content, exact location/size and generator settings. The current safety limit is 2048 resident tiles; exceeding it reports an error instead of silently losing roads. Trees and rocks use conservative road clearance during placement.

`RoadBuilder` on MapBuilder constructs owned texture-array, lookup and distant-coverage resources inside the same transactional construction root. The two TerrainLayer references provide repeating diffuse/normal textures and normal strength; they are surface profiles, not additional low-resolution biome splat layers. The large road uses the Rock source at fine scale for compact gravel; the medium road retains Earth with directional wear and a more muted tint. Both profiles can be replaced in the Inspector. Class changes taper the large ribbon and blend its appearance toward the adjoining smaller road. Built road textures survive MapData.Release and are released with their construction. The custom Terrain main/add/base passes blend the same world-space masks, and all grass representations/flowers exclude the road. No per-road GameObjects or separate road colliders are created. Like the existing grass facade, shader bindings target one active tactical map.

Near coverage has bounded edge filtering and shared irregular edges for ground/grass. A separate 32 m area-average coverage texture with mipmaps handles large pixel footprints. Empty space remains zero at every mip: the old distance sentinel can never be interpreted as road at a grazing horizon. The atlas RG channels contain class distances; B contains tangent angle and A contains the type-transition blend.

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Set Up Roads` installs/repairs source import, scene components, resource profiles and the existing workbench overlays without rebuilding the UI. Large roads appear ochre-brown and medium roads dark earth-brown on the overview and selected-source pixels; the red/green source encoding stays unchanged. The Roads checkbox affects preview visibility only. Generate Roads independently controls tactical generation. `Verify Roads` checks both classes, flat-ground bends, sparse distant coverage, width transitions, determinism, cancellation, hill avoidance, water gaps, disabling, object exclusion and resource lifetime; report: `Logs/Roads/checks.txt`. `Measure Road Generation` measures two complete map generations on the current selection and reports road capture, topology wait, routing and rasterization/cache times in `Logs/Roads/generation-timing.txt`.

## Low-poly meadow

After importing the UnityPackage, double-click `Install Grass Rendering.asset` in the MapGenerator folder, or open `Tools > Tactical Maps > Install Grass Render Feature...`. Select an Assets folder containing URP Renderer Data and click Install / Update. The installer searches subfolders, adds or repairs Grass Render Optimization, assigns its shader and owning Renderer Data, and saves only those renderer assets. Repeated installation does not create duplicate features. Existing quality settings and other renderer features are preserved. Export the entire MapGenerator folder, including Editor scripts, the installer asset and its stylesheet/shader dependencies. The installer does not require this project's PC_Renderer asset or the Unity CLI.

Rendering uses compact blade templates and separate GPU lists containing only flower-bearing plants. The original combined mesh assets remain the reference/bake topology, so their Inspector triangle counts include heads; live blade meshes contain 36/12/6 near triangles and 32/20 understorey triangles. Resolved plants retain the original blades; the new small-patch LODs use broader blades to preserve coverage. Flower sites, configured density, ranges and graphics quality settings are preserved. Per-view compute work prepares palette, family, morph, gust and understorey weights once per visible plant for all color/depth/normal passes. The 16-byte prepared cache uses binary16 for colors and bounded weights, float32 for wind, and no CPU readback. Dedicated near/understorey/head shader variants remove unused work. `Grass.PreparePlants` and `Grass.CullPlants` GPU markers expose work outside ExecuteRenderGraph. The conservative culling bound rejects plants entirely outside their shared handoff mask. Close blades retain their original topology; distant blades simplify across 6-10 projected pixels.

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Verify Grass Optimization` compares the original combined-mesh path and optimized path on identical frozen-wind cameras, checks sparse flower lists and flower toggling, and saves captures/timings in `Logs/GrassOptimization/Verified`. Its synchronized Editor timings include submission/readback and are not gameplay FPS. The internal reference switch is diagnostic only and defaults to the optimized path. `OptimizationForChecks` independently enables small near patches (1), small meadow patches (2), eight-triangle cards (8), and the combined pass (64); its default is 75. Setting it to zero retains the first optimization stage for paired comparisons.

MainScene has a separate `GrassSystem` object. Disable its component to release GPU buffers and disable the Terrain meadow material. Rocks and trees remain disabled. Implementation is isolated in `Runtime/Grass`; no per-plant GameObjects, colliders or materials are created.

The testing UI holds separate references to the generator, builder and grass system. The integration sequence is:

```csharp
MapData map = await generator.GenerateAsync(sourcePoint, cancellationToken);
mapBuilder.Build(map);
grassSystem.Build(map);
```

Grass resource/appearance/LOD settings remain private serialized Inspector fields. Rendering, exclusions, flower toggles, caches and lifecycle helpers are encapsulated. No task wrapper is needed for Build: Unity Terrain, textures and component binding must run on the main thread; per-camera GPU caches fill progressively during rendering.

The surface exists independently of geometry. `GrassTurfBaker` renders the actual GPU plants in an isolated preview scene to linear albedo/coverage and normal textures. Directional light is excluded from this bake. The textures have periodic edges, mipmaps and anisotropic filtering, and are integrated into the matte Terrain main/additive/basemap shaders. Two rotated texture scales reduce repetition. Coverage comes from existing biomes; shared world-space fields control freshness, dryness, color groups and wind. No global grass source map is required. The far surface provides coverage and lighting detail, not a silhouette above the horizon.

Three independently bounded geometry pools follow each camera. Near plants use 16 m cells with 64 x 64 candidates, up to 416 resident cells and a 144 m horizontal range (nominal fade starts at 84 m). A common 12-blade topology is reshaped on the GPU into low grass, tall stems and broad leaves, with independent roots, widths, curvature and height per blade. Configurable low-poly flower forms occur in coherent patches, with a shared palette across detailed/simplified geometry, understorey, cards and Terrain. Species randomness is independent of the rank used to thin instances. Density is independent of camera inclination. The detailed 36-triangle blade template morphs into a 12-triangle version of the same 12 blades at approximately 6-10 projected pixels. Midpoint positions, interpolated normals and wind converge to the same triangle before a single topology is selected; each plant is submitted once. Below 4.5 biased projected pixels, a six-blade template replaces the twelve tiny blades with twice-width leaves. Understorey patches whose tallest ordinary stem is below 3.5 pixels use 20 instead of 32 blades with 1.6 times the width; flower stems keep their actual width. Roots, heights and palette remain stable for retained blades. Flowers are always submitted independently of blade simplification. Projected size uses Euclidean camera distance, FOV and resolution, independent of camera orientation. Stable per-plant and regional threshold variation breaks up aligned transition fronts. Range-fade endpoints vary coherently across 82-100% of the configured radius, using one world-space field shared by adjacent representations. The coverage region is centered between the rendering camera and its viewed ground, using an iterative Terrain ray/height intersection with a bounded horizon fallback. For the main Game view this is the real Camera.main driven by Cinemachine, including blends and virtual-camera switches; no Follow/LookAt target is serialized. Scene and auxiliary views use their own rendering camera.

The medium understorey uses 64 m cells with 40 x 40 candidates, up to 224 cells and a 420 m range. Each shared patch has 16 broad low leaves, 16 upright stems and up to two flower heads (32 blade triangles plus up to two sparse 9-triangle flower heads) spread across its footprint; roots sample the actual built Terrain heights. Shared near-range weights modestly increase distant stem height as the near plants fade, retaining meadow volume. It is a cheaper layer of meadow mass, rather than a sparse substitute for the near plants. Both layers are opaque/double-sided, receive main-light shadows and cast no shadows. A directional sun is evaluated without per-Renderer distance attenuation, which is not populated for indirect draws. Ambient-probe colors are captured when a view cache is created.

An additional distant group layer bridges the orange understorey and blue Terrain surface. Each stable world-space group uses two crossed upright cards and two inclined top cards (eight triangles total; the ten-triangle authoring mesh remains intact), with four side/top variants baked from the actual detailed grass. Baked albedo has no directional lighting; baked normals are rotated with the group and lit at runtime. Coverage-weighted mip filtering prevents black fringes, and representative group normals retain soft distant lighting. Cards follow actual Terrain heights, use the same biome/beach/exclusion masks, receive shadows, cast none, and never face the camera. There are no per-group GameObjects. The independent pool uses 128 m cells, 40 x 40 candidates, at most 320 cells and a 900 m range. It appears across the existing understorey fade, then gradually yields to Terrain. Its debug color is purple. Distant lighting blends toward an upward diffuse canopy while retaining a little leaf-normal contrast and the baked texture detail; resolved close blades retain their original lighting. This layer is optional until its bake exists, so older libraries retain their original rendering.

The default GPU buffer reservation is about 142.1 MiB per camera, up to 284.2 MiB for two active Game/Scene views, plus shared textures (the new albedo/normal atlases add about 5.3 MiB). Extra simultaneous cameras retain the Terrain representation until an existing cache becomes idle. CPU frustum rejection precedes GPU instance culling. Stationary views reuse visibility; no normal-loop GPU readback or matrix upload occurs. At most eight new cells per layer per rendered frame are prepared. A two-cell near prefetch ring loads plants outside the visible range. Spare cells retain recently visited terrain; a slot is recycled only after its entire cell lies beyond the visible range. New plants have stable individual delays (0-0.18 s) and a 0.55 s fade. Range handoffs use complementary intervals of one fixed pixel rank shared by all geometry pools, with smoothly varying world-space thresholds. The orange/purple handoff therefore replaces coverage instead of adding a second independently fading layer. Nearby resolved blades retain their original understorey; it is progressively handed over as blades become small on screen. Screen-size and streaming fades retain the per-blade masks. Every clip is shared by color/depth/normal passes, without frame jitter. Each camera submits at most eight indirect draw commands (six blade/card lists and two sparse flower lists). `Grass Render Optimization` on PC_Renderer draws them once into depth, normals and an RGBA16F prelit color texture after the opaque depth/normal prepass and before SSAO. After opaque objects, one fullscreen composite restores grass color and writes its depth with a depth test, preserving object occlusion and transparent ordering. Shared color functions retain the existing main light, shadows, fog and canopy lighting. The temporary color target costs 11.0 MiB at 1600x900 (8 bytes/pixel), separate from persistent buffers and the 20 MiB flower-site cache. `Grass.LitNormals` and `Grass.Composite` identify these passes. Unsupported MSAA/stereo/overlay/compatibility-mode/deferred/depth-priming cameras, bakes, disabled/missing features and the first renderer-discovery frame use ordinary URP grass passes. Increasing range requires a suitable residency budget.

`MapGenerator` packs biome weights into cancellable CPU coverage fields in `MapData.Grass`. `MapBuilder.Build(map)` builds only Terrain, water, rocks and trees, then internally records the successful construction root in that MapData. `GrassSystem.Build(map)` is the single public grass operation: it copies coverage into built-map-owned GrassMap resources and binds the rendered grass. Call it on the Unity main thread after construction, with the same MapData. It targets the latest successful build of that result, without searching the scene for arbitrary Terrain. Unbuilt, released or destroyed-map results are rejected. Repeating the grass call replaces owned masks without duplicating components or rebuilding Terrain; preparation failures preserve existing grass. Construction failures preserve the preceding complete map; if a later grass build fails after successful construction, the newly built Terrain remains available. Built coverage survives `MapData.Release()`. The coverage blue channel stores sand weight; a shared 3-18% sand transition excludes the visible beach from geometry and Terrain turf. Every blade root samples this permission, including wide understorey patches. Persistent assets are preserved during cleanup. The internal `Exclude(Bounds)` helper suppresses an XZ region at coverage-map resolution, using private texture snapshots and refilling the geometry caches; it is not a fine road-painting tool.

The Show Flowers checkbox in the Flowers section on GrassSystem enables/disables flowers across all geometry LODs and Terrain, retaining the palette, density and LOD tuning. Its implementation is internal; the public grass facade exposes only Build(MapData). Disabled views skip/release the head caches; reenabling regenerates packed flower-bearing instances. The Flowers section also controls density, patch size, head radius and up to eight flower types (name, color, relative weight, head size and form). The four forms differ in actual geometry: an eight-petal flat rosette, a five-petal raised cup, a five-petal drooping bell and a narrow six-ray star, each with a contrasting center. Defaults are warm white, gold, violet and sparse red. Flower Density remains 0.035; it is not raised for visibility.

All representations share deterministic jittered one-metre head sites. A conservative 14-candidate density factor replaces the previous 16 near grass candidates per square metre. The near grass cell owning a site carries its flower; coarse grass retains up to two sites inside its own cell. Species/site indices and quantized facing fit in the existing 32-byte instance word, with the additional prepared-data and sparse-head buffers included in the reservation above.

Terrain main/add/base materials paint individual world-space head silhouettes directly in albedo. Existing distant groups project the same heads onto a shared canopy plane along each pixel ray, so upright cards no longer hide them or stretch them into stripes. Flower coverage survives the grass alpha cutout, and flower color receives canopy lighting without multiplication by the blade texture. This does not create flower GameObjects, extra meshes, renderers or draw calls. Screen-space antialiasing and bounded head-size compensation keep marks legible. When ground cells become subpixel, smoothly blended spatial subsets retain real sites rather than accumulating enlarged heads into a colored carpet. Each of at most two active views owns a 1024 x 1024 x 5 RGBA8 nearest-head cache (20 MiB per view, 40 MiB maximum), independently of camera instance buffers. Separate windows prevent Game and Scene cameras from repeatedly invalidating each other. Each slice uses a world-aligned toroidal lattice; camera movement rebuilds only newly exposed strips in bounded dispatches. Stationary cameras reuse all data; changing density, seed, patch size or palette weights invalidates the sites. Colors, forms, radii and sizes remain live shader inputs. Materials normally read one or two cached heads instead of evaluating up to eighteen procedural neighbours per pixel; an out-of-cache horizon retains deterministic procedural fallback. Purple cards retain 50% of shared sites by a stable world hash; the underlying Terrain follows the same thinning through the orange-to-purple handoff. The far Terrain retains 55% of shared sites (45% fewer) and reduces head radius to 2/3 through the purple-to-Terrain handoff; near and orange flower density stay unchanged. GrassSystem exposes Purple Flower Retention, Distant Flower Retention, Distant Flower Size and Distant Flower Maximum Distance. The default Terrain flower limit is 3000 m from the rendering camera, with a smooth fade over the final 10%; pixels beyond it skip head cache lookups and procedural fallback. The older R8 petal bakes remain diagnostic/reference resources; they are no longer the runtime distant-head representation.

Inspector settings control density, height range, slope/coast restrictions, palette, wind, ranges, residency and diagnostics. Debug View 1 shows coverage, 2 shows detailed green, simplified yellow, understorey orange, distant groups purple and Terrain blue and 3 shows logical cells. There is no CPU wind simulation.

## Runtime groups

| Folder | Namespace | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Runtime/Testing` | `TacticalMaps.Testing` | Temporary `MapWorkbench` controller and selection UI |
| `Runtime/Generation` | `TacticalMaps.Generation` | Source data, settings, height calculation and output textures |
| `Runtime/Construction` | `TacticalMaps.Construction` | Terrain and water spawning, appearance, overlap and resource lifetime |

`MapBuilder` exposes exactly one entry point: `public void Build(MapData map)`. All its other methods and fields are private. It has no references to the generator or controller; `MapWorkbench` supplies finished data. MapBuilder owns the common center and completed map lifetime. TerrainGenerator owns terrain layer, overlap, rendering error, terrain material and detail distance. WaterGenerator owns the water layer, material and mesh density. Both generators are on the same object and are called only by MapBuilder. Build is transactional: a failed stage keeps the previous map and cleans its partial replacement.

The three centered terrains preserve their individual world dimensions and a shared vertical scale. Unity Terrain requires a square height grid of `2^n + 1` samples; a rectangular input is bilinearly resampled to the next supported square resolution, without changing the input texture or the rectangular world dimensions. Background terrain interiors are lowered and cut out beneath the previous level, retaining an overlap band. Only the main terrain collider is enabled. Rebuilding replaces only this builder's previous output and releases its transient TerrainData; saved project assets are preserved.

MainScene contains an initial built set with saved data in `Generated/Terrains`. `Tools > Tactical Maps > Set Up Terrain Construction` installs construction in a workbench scene and saves an initial preview. `Tools > Tactical Maps > Verify Terrain Construction` checks dimensions, height orientation, rectangular resampling, holes, collider selection, replacement, cleanup and the single-entry-point API in a preview scene.

## Coordinates and scale

- Source coordinates start at the bottom left, matching Unity texture coordinates.
- One radius controls the shared source square: radius 50 gives 101 × 101 samples.
- The entire square maps to level III. Levels I and II sample its center according to their world dimensions relative to level III.
- Each level has independent texture width/height and world width/height. Dimensions need not be square. Every next world rectangle must contain the previous one.
- Initial textures are 2049 × 2049, with world sizes 2048, 8096 and 20000 metres.
- A pixel outside the source image is water; the selection is not shifted at the image edges.

## Heights

The source heightmap is imported as readable, linear, uncompressed R16 at its full 4096 × 4096 resolution. The land mask uses R8; white is land. Source images themselves are unchanged.

All outputs use linear R16 and one shared vertical range. `TerrainHeightMeters` is the physical range from base to heightmap value 1; `SeaLevelMeters` must be nonnegative and below that range. The global height is a source reference and no longer forces the Terrain range. Inland elevation is `sea + source * (terrainHeight - sea)`: source 0.7, Terrain 1000 and sea 50 give 715 metres before smoothing/compression. Terrain 1000 with global reference 4000 is valid. No per-level normalization is applied.

Water-mask samples ignore the global heightmap. The seabed starts near sea level at the shore and descends smoothly to zero with distance into water. `Seabed Pixels` controls its width in source pixels; a wider ramp is used when necessary to reserve the main terrain's slope budget. `Coast Pixels` controls the above-water coast transition. Gaussian smoothing and compression affect the main land relief; a transition restores background relief outside the main rectangle. Water depth remains a shared reference for all levels. Coincident world coordinates agree, with the construction overlap band handling resampling differences.

Preview brightness affects only rendering, not stored heights. The crop grid uses point filtering; green/orange/blue rectangles identify the three footprints. Dense grids are hidden above 151 samples per side to keep the preview readable.

Runtime regeneration runs on a background task. Changing the selection cancels the previous result, waits for a short drag debounce and generates the latest selection. Unity textures are created on the main thread. Play-mode parameter changes follow normal Unity behavior and reset on leaving Play mode.

## Files and checks

`Generated/Heightmap1.asset` through `Heightmap3.asset` contain the saved initial previews. The root object `MapGenerator` owns input maps and generation settings. `internal MapData Generate(Vector2Int point)` returns a completed map; `GenerateAsync(point, cancellation)` returns `Task<MapData>`. The point is an argument, never a generation setting. The generator stores no generated textures or current result. Its colocated `HeightmapGenerator` component implements the height stage and source cache; only MapGenerator calls that component. Further map-generation stages belong behind the same facade.

`MapData` is a public result class: properties use internal getters and private setters. It contains three read-only heightmap references, read-only world sizes, the common terrain height, sea level, global elevation reference, selected source point and compression metadata. Texture dimensions are read from the textures. Results remain valid across later generations and generator destruction. The caller owns transient textures and calls `map.Release()` when finished; the operation is idempotent and preserves saved assets.

`MapWorkbench` owns the current returned map and selection point. It orchestrates MapGenerator, MapBuilder and GrassSystem in that order, replaces and releases old results, and releases canceled/stale results. `MapSelectionGraphic` handles selection outlines and pointer input. `HeightField` contains the numerical algorithm, and `MapSettings` contains serialized configuration. The test controller and implementation helper types are internal; configurable fields are `[SerializeField] private`. Settings are copied when read or configured, so callers cannot silently mutate a running generation. Editor tools access internal methods through `InternalsVisibleTo("Assembly-CSharp-Editor")`; inspector references are assigned through `SerializedObject`.

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Verify Height Generation` checks water override, rectangular output, shared coordinates, slope, transitions, source borders and cancellation. It also checks standalone generation, private configuration, internal result getters/private setters, independent result lifetime, point arguments, defensive settings copies, nonzero-sea coast slopes and proportional scaling into different Terrain ranges and a seabed that deepens away from shore.

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Create MainScene Workbench` builds the interface, generator and construction object in an open MainScene that has no workbench yet. It refuses to overwrite an existing workbench. Scene creation uses Unity Editor APIs.

Unity Pipeline is included to allow editor automation; TMP Essentials and a local Arial font asset provide the Russian interface.

## Water

`Runtime/Construction` contains MapBuilder (public facade with one public `Build(MapData)` method), TerrainGenerator and WaterGenerator (internal stages). Inspector layer fields are actual layer dropdowns, not LayerMasks. Existing project layers Terrain and Water are used; no camera or input code is required by the package.

`Materials/LowPolyWater.mat` uses `Shaders/LowPolyWater.shader` for URP. The water is a single continuous planar mesh covering the outer map. The axis spacing is denser in the main region and coarser in the middle/outer bands, without separate LOD surfaces or seams. Default settings give 31,329 vertices and 61,952 triangles in one transparent forward pass. Water has no collider, reflection camera, shadow-casting pass, screen-depth requirement, refraction or CPU Update animation. These are geometry/draw budgets, not a measured frame-rate guarantee.

Two waves displace vertices on the GPU. Fragment derivatives supply flat triangle normals for the low-poly style. The material controls amplitude, wavelength, speed, shallow/deep colors, approximate sky tint, sun glints and animated shoreline foam. The mesh bounds allow displacement. Wave amplitude fades near shore; water fragments with effectively zero depth are clipped. A small surface offset avoids coincident surfaces. Sea level zero has no physical water depth; use a positive level for visible water and its depth-based transparency, color and foam.

Water snapshots the three heightmaps at up to 1025 / 513 / 257 pixels per axis (about 2.8 MB of R16 texels for square defaults). They provide world-space seabed depth and land clipping without changing renderer or camera settings. The snapshots and a per-built-map material belong to MapBuilder; later MapData.Release calls cannot invalidate water. Rebuilds dispose the previous transient meshes, terrain data, material and snapshots, while preserving saved assets.

MainScene has a saved preview in `Generated/MapPreview`. Change the source water material, then rebuild to apply it to a new surface. Test controls generate MapData, call MapBuilder.Build(map), then GrassSystem.Build(map). All implementation and generated assets remain under Assets/MapGenerator.

## Terrain surfaces and biomes

`BiomeGenerator` is an internal component on the MapGenerator object. Only the facade invokes it. Its private serialized layer references and settings control six replaceable surfaces: meadow, dry grass, earth, rock, sand and silt. Settings cover moisture, coast influence, altitude dryness, valley radius/moisture, regional climate, erosion slopes, rock slopes, beach height, silt depth, seed and splat resolutions. Regional randomness is only a subtle climate bias; height, gradient, aspect and topographic position select coverage. There is no Perlin color pattern.

MapData.Surface contains the shared layer library, three normalized splat arrays and common texture origin. Async generation snapshots height pixels on the main thread and computes weights on a worker with cancellation. TerrainGenerator uploads these finished weights and clones TerrainLayers with aligned texture phase; it performs no biome calculation. MapBuilder owns the layer clones, terrain data and water resources; albedo/normal textures belong to the shared asset library and survive rebuilds.

`Textures/Surfaces` holds six generated albedos plus normal-map import sources. `Materials/TerrainLayers` holds their editable TerrainLayer assets. Change a layer's albedo, normal map, normal strength, tint/remap, smoothness and Tile Size directly; tile dimensions are metres (defaults about 6–13 m), independent of terrain size. Mipmaps, anisotropic filtering and normals keep close views detailed and reduce shimmer. Slightly different tile scales and blending of related surfaces reduce visible repetition. No geometry grass is spawned.

The main splat map is 1024 square, backgrounds 512. These maps control material coverage, not fine surface detail. The matte terrain shader reuses URP terrain instancing, holes, lighting and shadows. Six layers can require two terrain layer passes; these are conventional Terrain materials, not a one-pass or measured FPS guarantee. `Detailed Surface Distance` on TerrainGenerator controls the distance before Unity's cheaper far basemap.

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Set Up Terrain Surface Library` configures/imports the six albedo/normal pairs and assigns the generation component. It also enables animated material updates in the active Scene View. `Rebuild Saved Map Preview` regenerates the current selection and saves MainScene. Existing generator height settings and camera setup are preserved.

Water now combines GPU vertex waves, drifting fine ripple normals, moving crest highlights and irregular shore foam. The material exposes Ripple Size/Strength, Flow Speed and Ripple Highlights as well as the original wave settings. Transparent blending and depth-dependent opacity remain. In Scene View, animated materials and Always Refresh must be enabled to see continuous motion outside Play Mode; the setup tool enables them. Runtime animation uses shader time with no per-frame CPU mesh updates.

`Verify Biome Generation` checks deterministic normalized weights, coast/bed/slope rules, rectangular dimensions and cancellation. `Verify Water Motion` renders two fixed-camera frames and checks their pixel difference in active Edit Mode or unpaused Play Mode. Keep Unity foreground while running the check so the Editor updates its rendering loop. `Capture Surface Preview` writes a close view to Temp for visual inspection. All three testing tools are editor-only and create temporary verification cameras; the user's camera controller is unchanged.

Generated albedos use the built-in image_gen tool. The prompt set is saved in `Textures/Surfaces/GenerationPrompts.md`.

## Tree groves and GPU instancing

`TreePlacementGenerator` computes separate forest masks and points for all three terrain levels. Maximum Coverage defaults to 0.30: the main mask is capped at 30% of its complete rectangle, while each background mask is capped at 30% of its visible ring area. Water remains in the denominator. Inner rectangles plus Seam Clearance (120 m) are excluded on background levels, avoiding duplicate trees and the terrain blending band. Unsuitable land and small-zone removal can reduce coverage. Zone metadata reports occupied cells as a fraction of the whole level rectangle.

The Inspector separates zone coverage, grove size and point density. Main defaults: Zone Cell Size 12 m, grove radii 65–180 m, Minimum Grove Area 2000 m², Tree Spacing 10 m (minimum trunk distance 7 m), Maximum Slope 28°, Shore Clearance 12 m. Background Spacing multipliers 1.6/2.4 give 16/24 m; Background Grove Scale 2/4 gives larger groves and 24/48 m analysis cells. Count caps are 25000 / 60000 / 120000, independently adjustable. Disabling Generate Background Trees retains only the main forest.

Irregular groves and ranked equal-area masks enforce the area budget. A spatial grove lookup avoids scanning every grove for every cell on large maps. Jittered candidates, local density variation, minimum-distance rejection and a minimum connected group of three prevent isolated trees. Group IDs are local to each terrain level. Seed/source point determine the result; water, steep slopes and projected rock bounds exclude planting. Main-thread snapshots are followed by cancellable worker computation. `MapData.Trees.Zones` and placements remain independent of rendering.

`TreeBuilder` saves placements as native TreePrototype/TreeInstance arrays on all three TerrainData objects and snaps trunks to the actual built heights. It disables `Terrain.drawTreesAndFoliage` and creates one `GeneratedMap/Trees` object with `InstancedTreeRenderer`. Close trees use **Graphics.RenderMeshInstanced** mesh/material batches of at most 511 instances; distant trees use a two-triangle camera-facing impostor with **Graphics.RenderMeshIndirect**. Nonuniform instance scaling is retained. No GameObject, MeshRenderer, material clone or Update is created per tree. TerrainData retains the editable placement source; after manual Paint Trees edits, rebuild the renderer to refresh its cache.

`Assets/Prefabs/Trees/TreeRenderLibrary.asset` contains four runtime mesh levels per species plus the distant impostor, and retains the original Synty mesh/material levels for authoring and A/B profiling. The originals are not modified. The optimized meshes/materials live under `Assets/Prefabs/Trees/Optimized`; they are direct runtime records and do not need per-tree prefab instances.

`Optimize Tree Meshes And Transitions` regenerates all six species and updates the current TreeBuilder/InstancedTreeRenderer threshold. Generation starts from the authored mesh LODs, preserves whole connected leaf cards, distributes a bounded subset across the crown with deterministic farthest-point selection, and enlarges retained cards to preserve coverage. Ordinary MeshLodUtility simplification is applied separately to wood. Attributes, UVs, root transforms and wind masks are retained. Generated ranges are compact ordinary meshes, so runtime rendering does not depend on automatic native Mesh LOD selection. Repeated generation updates existing asset GUIDs and removes obsolete generated meshes.

The current triangle chains are Pine_01: 15588 / 6100 / 1952 / 1278; Pine_02: 3888 / 1624 / 696 / 306; Pine_03: 1888 / 904 / 392 / 304; Maple_01: 14164 / 5568 / 1696 / 790; Maple_02: 594 / 530 / 124 / 104; Ginko_01: 6280 / 3086 / 1560 / 824. Budgets are quality constrained: complete cards and a conservative minimum crown density take priority over an exact triangle count.

Close and middle trees use `TacticalMaps/Trees/Foliage`: one coherent leaf/trunk texture sample, vertex tint/frosting, simple vertex wind, diffuse Forward/Forward+ lighting, shadows and SSAO. Only full and near levels retain optional leaf normal mapping. The shader omits the original procedural pixel color noise, multiple wind modes, metallic/specular and extra surface maps. Depth and ShadowCaster passes share alpha clipping and wind. Blue vertex color > 0.5 selects leaves, matching the source art.

Mesh LOD transitions and mesh/impostor transitions use complementary stable screen-space dithering over a narrow +/-12% size band. Per-instance coverage travels through cached MaterialPropertyBlock arrays; a transition submits at most two visible representations. Stationary cameras reuse their batches. Mesh Screen Height defaults to 0.13 and is compared with the size estimate multiplied by QualitySettings.lodBias; at bias 2 this is roughly 6.5% of the screen, not 13%. Mesh thresholds are 0.95 / 0.36 / 0.18 / 0.006. If an edited impostor threshold would overlap a mesh transition, unreachable lower mesh levels are skipped consistently. Lowering the impostor threshold keeps more geometry and can increase distant-view cost.

Visible optimized meshes do not cast a second copy of their shadow. When Cast Shadows is enabled, the renderer submits a separate coarse level (normally generated LOD2) with ShadowsOnly within the effective range. Offscreen nearby casters remain supported. Tree shadow distance is clamped by the pipeline's range and the camera's shadow setting; disabling pipeline shadows also stops submission of the shadow meshes. Distant impostors do not cast shadows.

Impostors remain two-triangle quads with 24 baked views (8 azimuths x 3 elevations) in 2048x768 atlases. The baker uses the optimized full-mesh materials with wind disabled and the scene sun when assigned. Lighting is static on this path; rebake after substantial lighting/art changes. CPU mesh and GPU impostor culling overlap in the same transition band. Impostor fragments apply the complementary mask. Minimum Impostor Pixels remains 1.5 and Tree Distance 6000 m.

GPU instance buffers are uploaded once, culling/count compaction stays on the GPU, and diagnostic readback is outside the render loop. Staged maps allocate no GPU resources until first rendering; disable/rebuild releases buffers. Up to four camera views are cached. A system without compute support uses the generated mesh chain, including its mesh-to-mesh dithering and final small-object fade.

Editor tools under `Tools > Tactical Maps`:

- `Configure And Rebuild Trees`: regenerate tree placements on the existing three terrains without rebuilding heights, water or rocks; retain a current optimized library.
- `Optimize Tree Meshes And Transitions`: regenerate mesh LODs, materials and atlases, and apply the tuned threshold to the current scene. Save the scene to persist the component change.
- `Bake Optimized Tree LODs`: regenerate optimized meshes/materials and bake all impostor atlases.
- `Verify Optimized Tree LODs`: test all six species at 11 screen sizes, editable thresholds, shader compilation, fade overlap, stationary cache, disable/enable and mesh-only fallback. Contact sheet and report: `Temp/tree-lod-contact-sheet.png`, `Temp/tree-lod-checks.txt`.
- `Compare Tree Optimization`: compare preserved original mesh LODs with the old 0.20 hybrid threshold against the optimized path, on identical game/near/canopy/grove/wide views. Reports/images: `Temp/tree-cost-hybrid-before*` and `Temp/tree-cost-hybrid-after*`. Both states use the current baked far atlases. Render/readback times include CPU, GPU and synchronization, not isolated GPU timings.
- `Verify Tree Placement` and `Verify And Profile Tree Rendering`: retain placement, rebuild, ownership, all-three-terrain and culling integration checks; original authoring references are checked separately from generated levels.
- `Build Tree Performance Check`: save MainScene and build a development player under `Builds/TreeOptimized`. Starting that player with `-tree-benchmark -screen-fullscreen 0 -screen-width 1600 -screen-height 900` runs an opt-in benchmark on the saved map and exits. `Temp/tree-player/report.txt` contains GPU/CPU frame medians and p95 with screenshots; each state has 60 warm-up frames and 180 measurement frames with no synchronous readback. Normal launches create no benchmark component. GPU timings may be unavailable on an unsupported graphics backend.

## Shadow performance profiles

`Tools > Tactical Maps > Shadows` provides object-shadow, tree-shadow, reference-settings and fully disabled profiles. Profiles update both the current generated map and its builders, so rebuilding the map retains the selected casting policy. Save the scene after applying a profile.

The Performance profile uses a 200 m range, one cascade, a 2048 atlas, hard shadows, a 20% distance fade, one-sided Terrain casting and no tree casting. Balanced uses two cascades and Soft Low. Buildings and other ordinary casters retain their shadows. `TreeBuilder` and `InstancedTreeRenderer` expose Cast Shadows independently; profiles align their optional casting distance with the pipeline fade. The disabled profile retains the Performance settings and switches pipeline shadow support off.

Grass never casts shadows. GrassSystem's Shadow Filtering controls receiving: Off, Hard (one hardware-filtered compare) or Soft (the light's PCF filtering). Performance and Balanced use Hard for grass; Balanced retains soft shadows for Terrain/objects. Geometry density, LOD ranges and flower density are unchanged. Grass and tree receiving now apply the same distance fade as URP; fragments beyond the range skip shadow-map filtering. Terrain uses an equivalent early exit while preserving baked-shadow/GI mixing, cookies and AO. The native URP package is not edited.

`Verify in Game View` temporarily enters Play Mode, samples advancing frames on the saved map, captures camera motion and restores the pipeline, profiler, background-update and Game View settings on exit. It disables workbench regeneration for the test. GPU pass times are collected only without Graphics Jobs, which Unity 6.3 requires; native-job runs report ordinary Editor frame times instead. Reports/screenshots are under `Logs/ShadowGameView`. The opt-in development-player `-shadow-benchmark -shadow-visible` probe supports distance, cascades, atlas and hard/soft comparisons. Keep its window visible: completed camera callbacks verify every rendered frame. It reports uninstrumented player frame elapsed; GPU measurements are separate. `-shadow-render-request` is an optional offscreen diagnostic, whose timings must not be interpreted as ordinary gameplay FPS. Reference/receiver-ablation globals remain zero in normal rendering.

## Matte terrain and stochastic texturing

`Materials/MatteTerrain.mat` is the source material used by TerrainGenerator. It uses `TacticalMaps/Terrain/MatteStochastic`, with custom additive and distant matte shaders. Lighting is diffuse-only: no specular, Fresnel or environment-reflection term, regardless of TerrainLayer smoothness or texture alpha. Detail normals, directional/additional lights, shadows, fog and ambient lighting remain.

Three hashed samples on a triangular world-space lattice randomize texture translation and quarter-turn orientation. Albedo and normal samples share transforms and weights; normal XY is rotated back into the original tangent frame. Explicit rotated texture gradients retain anisotropic mip filtering at grazing angles. Variance correction preserves fine contrast across blend regions. The lattice scale is independent of terrain dimensions, and each terrain material stores its physical size so nested levels retain the same lattice after saving/reopening.

The source material exposes Anti-tiling Strength, Patch Width (in texture tiles), Blend Sharpness, Pattern Seed, Anti-tiling Fade Start/End (metres), and Detail Normal Strength. Defaults fade stochastic sampling from 1200 to 3000 metres; beyond that a single ordinary sample per map is used. During the fade an additional regular sample allows a continuous transition. Unused layers are skipped where their splat weights are zero. These are sampling costs, not a guaranteed frame-rate result.

TerrainGenerator keeps geometric LOD (`Main Pixel Error = 1`). `Main Surface Distance` defaults to 12000 m, with a separate 6000 m background distance. This controls material basemaps, not mesh simplification. Surface imports use anisotropy 16 and ordinary mip bias 0. The distant basemap has its own diffuse-only shader so gloss cannot reappear there.

Each built terrain owns a material clone; source materials/textures remain shared assets. Rebuild/rollback releases transient clones. `Tools > Tactical Maps > Set Up Matte Terrain` updates the saved scene materials without regenerating heightmaps or water. Rebuild after editing source-material settings to update all three clones.

`Verify Matte Terrain` renders a solid glossy-input regression under high/low viewing angles, checks additive layers 5/6 and basemap visibility, and compares the tile-period difference with anti-tiling off/on. It also reports synchronized offscreen render/readback timings, which include CPU work and are not a pure GPU measurement. `Capture Matte Terrain Angles` writes top/grazing/distant images to Temp. Current Editor GPU frame timing was unavailable; profile target gameplay hardware before deciding the final quality distances.
